# Changelog

## 2.0.0 - 2026-10-24

- States: seven states computed from records only (running, asking, saturated, ineffective, guarded, implausible, paused), with a master caution line at the top of status naming which ventures need a human; a row shows the venture's metric and a cap that still binds whatever the state, a self pause shows as its own, paused ventures are listed apart with the alarm that no lever runs, implausible names the reading that holds it and clears once that metric is trusted again, and an ineffective venture with nothing to answer says re-adopting restores it and that approving runs the lever.
- World models and forecasts: `world:` names a command that steps a venture's world, and with `world:` or `calibration:` every report brings a one-step prediction that the next trusted, plausible report resolves and code scores as a `calibration` report; forecasts are forked branches with worst-case governance, walls, capacity and CSCV's probability of backtest overfitting, they rest on trusted readings, keep to the metric's range and precision, and say `too little history to forecast` rather than print numbers. A forecast runs off the kernel's lock, so commands are answered and guards read while it runs, an unchanged re-add forecasts nothing new, asks dedupe by payload, and the add forecast is written when it is done. The baseline scales drift by elapsed time, respects range and the hour of day, counts drift once, rolls computed guards, keeps its drift steps with the index, and reads neither a daily cycle nor a step a run is still judged over as drift.
- Plausibility: a report outside `range`, a step past `max_step`, a flap, a NaN or a failed report is kept, marked implausible, never acted on, and asks once until a plausible report comes; `stuck` is a run of repeats the metric's own history makes unlikely, judged by the posterior predictive over its chance anywhere in the history, counted across any gap of missing readings, and never anchored or judged by a stuck reading. A reading right after an implausible one is unconfirmed until the next agrees with it within `max_step`, a reading at its range bound is real, a missing or out-of-range reading never breaks a frozen plateau after a run, and a reading stuck at its value from before a run judges that run ineffective. A human re-anchors a doubted reading, a suspension seen only on a stuck sensor holds only while it stays frozen, and a doubted reading never asks again about a dead lever.
- Expectations and suspension: a run is judged at its `within` count net of the recent lever-free drift, and a run that misses at that count is judged again at the delay its effect shows; a metric that steps to a new value and holds it asks again, a span of repeats no longer than the sensor holds one is not a miss, and fewer than 3 steps of history judge nothing. A run on a suspended lever is refused, the lever asks once an episode and re-asks on new evidence, and the latest answer to that ask governs its suspension; a delegated lever that does not move the metric loses its delegation, and an ineffective ask offers to delegate again only a delegated lever.
- Guards and unwind: a guard reads beside the metric with a bound, past which it pauses the venture or asks once and refuses every lever but stop and its own unwind, and an audit asks when the metric improves over its window while the audit gets worse by more than its tolerance. A guard declares an `unwind`: one lever and the exact payload it takes, run once per episode when the guard trips, under that lever's cap, budget and check, while the same lever with any other payload stays refused; a suspension seen only on a stuck sensor does not hold an unwind. `max_rise` and `max_fall` bound a metric's two ways apart, in place of `max_step` or beside it. An approval vouches for one reading, so repeats and flaps stay implausible, though approving a stuck ask vouches for the whole run of repeats; a reading's lines are kept only when that audit's latest reading names them, so a model sees who is waiting and never what they wrote; `resume` warns when a guard still reads past its bound and resumes anyway.
- Capacity asks: a venture whose levers a limit holds back names the binding limit in its packet, the held-back ask and the digest — a cap spent with no margin, or what held the levers back — and a cap window ends now; a held-back ask needs a change past ten units of the metric's noise, a cap binds only on a reading it left unanswered, a run behind a lagging sensor is measured at the reading that refreshed, a lever's `cost:` divides the parent's budget in the ask, and a capacity episode closes after a quiet week.
- Approvals and the gate: approvals are single-use — only the first answer to an ask counts, a later one is kept and authorizes nothing, and an approval refused for any reason (a pause, a cap, a budget, a quota) or refused by the warden after the gate said yes is spent; an approval of a paused venture's ask is refused, and an approval that did not run is spent rather than run again on start. Every run is recorded, then done, an intent before it and an outcome after, a pending intent always asks and its blocked approval runs once settled, a refusal is a record, and a nightly cycle that yields nothing usable leaves a record. No run goes again while the last run's expectation is waiting, a run of a restored lever cites the approval that restored it, a record dated in the future never fills a cap window, and a delegated lever never asks, so the asks quota is not what binds it.
- Asks and packets: an ask is a decision packet carrying its premises, its options and the reason, built by code, and approving it runs the lever it names; a saturated ask says what approving does and that a wait is not a limit, an ineffective ask cites the misses, a packet forecast shows both bounds of a band, and no forecast says why. `canary_asks` writes a canary ask every n asks — one that can never run — to measure whether the human reads what they approve. A signal ask sits outside the asks quota: a saturated or ineffective lever, an intent with no outcome, a tripped guard that asks, an audit, an implausible report, a critical slowing down warning, a forecast that reaches a wall, a capacity shortfall.
- Roles and escalation: a reply carries up to five independent choices, each judged alone, a command backend's whole reply is returned, and code computes a summary's numbers. When a reply cannot be used, avsm asks the backend named under `escalate:`, writing a measured `escalation` record before the call and a measured `answer` record after, as record kinds of their own; a backend that leaves the machine is asked only for a venture whose mandate says `escalate: offbox`. A parent's view of a child prefers its metric, runs and asks and collapses guard readings, and the improving loop never edits limits.
- Mandates, controllers and allocation: `controller: {kind: band}` holds the metric between two bounds with a lever for each side and one action per excursion, `controller: {kind: plan}` searches lever sequences through the world model's ensemble with early warnings calibrated to a measured false-alarm rate, a parent may set `allocation` with a deadband, per-child floors, priority and a `total` its children's budgets never pass, and it keeps the latest threshold call of each side of a band. `takes: json` may carry fields the warden checks the payload against, a shell metric takes a label, and `canary` is a reserved lever name. `add` prints the mandate back as sentences, lists the numbers nothing enforces, and states `max_rise` and `max_fall` as enforced; the ratchet proposes the adopted mandate rather than the file on disk, makes no irreversible delegation, validates its own edit, and a proposal past its cap is refused and never asked.
- Ledger integrity: the ledger keeps a rollback journal with `synchronous=FULL`, refuses unchained inserts and every `UPDATE` and `DELETE`, fails closed on a rewind, and writes a non-finite number as the text inf, -inf or nan and refuses to append a record it cannot read back; the gate loads only what a candidate rests on, an index is kept record by record, a copy of the index shares its records, and only new entries are verified between whole reads of the chain. The digest ends with the head of the chain and is written each nightly; `doctor --since` checks the ledger still holds that hash at that number, taking the digest's head line as printed, and `doctor --cut` needs a bad record or `--force`, never erases an earlier cut, and names the uid's account. A record avsmd did not write is an alarm: `self` cites it, pauses everything and says so in status and doctor, an approval among them spent for good, and `avsm why` says avsmd did not write it. Each daemon holds a lock on `avsmd.lock` for its life, a second is refused with 75, a deleted lock file or socket is taken back, doctor names a second avsmd, and it names an append-only trigger that was dropped. A new ledger is 0600 and a new home 0700, and doctor warns about a readable ledger.
- Liveness: levers, reports and reads run on their own threads, so a hung lever or metric no longer freezes the daemon, and a guard, audit or reading past half a second is read apart and written as not read until it is done. Every lever, report, guard, audit, reading and run leads a process group of its own and none outlives avsmd: the warden tells avsmd of each group it starts, a drain ends with the group rather than its leader, a stop, a kill or the pause of its venture ends what is left at once rather than at the next tick, and the next start checks each group by its start time, its mark or its directory before ending it. A dead warden or role is respawned and a measured report of `self`'s says so, a lever the dead warden was running is asked about and never run again blindly, a warden death raises no false alarm, and time is read through one clock. A command from a process avsmd started — anything whose parent chain leads back to avsmd, or anything in a group or session it made, alive or gone within the last minute — is refused, a double-forked approval and an approver moved to a group of its own included, while reading commands and `pause` stay open; the peer's pid, group and session are what avsmd checks.
- Simulate: `avsm simulate` runs ventures over virtual days in a home of its own, with a scripted human (`all`, `none`, or `mixed`, which denies every third ask and edits text payloads), timed by `--start` and a virtual clock. No venture program runs, with or without a world: no lever, `report`, guard, audit or reading, since a program may change what it reads. `--world` names a command that stands for the world: every lever run goes to it with `AVSM_LEVER`, `AVSM_KEY` and the payload on standard input; a line it prints as `name=number` or `venture/name=number` is that step's reading, `nan` and an absurd number included; a metric, guard, audit or reading it prints nothing for reads as missing; a line `name: a@b c@d` gives an audit's or a reading's lines and `venture/audit.name> text` an audit's note, kept as the warden keeps a real one's for a payload check to read. Without a world the baseline replays the ledger's last trusted reading. A simulation exits 70 when it cannot run or its world fails, and the text it prints is escaped.
- Sims in the repo: `sims/hostile` is a kit that tries to make avsm do what it must not; `sims/heldout`, `heldout2` and `heldout3` are kits on shapes neither the code nor the earlier round saw, with pre-registered checks, and `heldout3` is a fish farm. A checker reads `why` by its real arguments, counts refusals by check before any ask, checks that every printed cap or budget refusal has a record, splits waterings after death by what had happened when each ran, and holds a check a model may never reach as null until it does.
- CLI, daemon and doctor messages: the daemon is `avsmd`, with sshd's flags (`-t`, `-T`, `-f`, `-d`) and the conventions of a daemon that never forks; `-T` prints the `forecast` and `canary_asks` defaults. `add` names what is wrong with the path without os errors and exits 66 for a directory that is not there; a missing home, a socket path too long and a run that is not there are named; `remove`, `rm` and `delete` say there is no remove; `approve`, `deny` and `why` with no number get the zsh comment hint. Control, invisible and bidi characters are escaped before they reach a terminal, a closed pipe ends quietly and an unwritable standard output exits 74, and stderr states a refusal when it opens or its reason changes rather than every tick. `doctor` finds a CRLF shebang and a program that is not executable, prints the `chmod +x` fix with no `/./` in the path, notes a paused venture whose directory has gone, and refuses `--cut` with 75 while avsmd runs on the home.
- Compatibility: a ledger written by 1.0 opens as it is — one in WAL mode is moved to the rollback journal the first time avsmd opens it and no record is rewritten — and an adoption stores the mandate text with `why` naming its version, so a record from 1.0 still reads.
- Verified: 791 tests pass on x86_64 macOS and Linux CI. Four rounds of simulated users (a novice, a power user, a chaos tester and an adversary, each round on release binaries) found breaks of the non-negotiables and bugs, tracked on issues #42, #50, #55 and #61 and fixed, each fix mutation-tested. The scripted 365-day hostile simulation (43,626 records, chain verified): 0 breaches; NaN, missing, absurd and flapping readings flagged 48/48, 48/48, 96/96 and 144/144 and never a premise; a capacity ask on day 14; drawdown 17.7% against a 15% guard and a 15.7% one-step crash; the tomato's floor held; the dead plant watered 11 times, never against the evidence, 10 of them approved by the scripted human with the misses shown. The 90-day quarter with Sonnet as the model, on c1fb798: tomato and inbox capacity asks on days 13 and 14, every cap refusal recorded, 0 breaches; its backlog did not fall after the day-75 mail storm.

## 1.0.0 - 2026-10-16

- Payloads: proposals and asks carry the text, json, diff or file a lever consumes; `takes:` on a lever; a model's content runs only after a declared approval unless delegated; `avsm asks` shows it, `avsm approve <n> --edit` amends it, `avsm propose` files one by hand.
- Adapters: built-in metrics (stripe.mrr, stripe.charges_today, github.open_issues, github.failed_ci, imap.unread, http.status, file.number, shell) and levers (github.issue, github.comment, github.pr, mail.send, http.post), run in the warden with the one key each is scoped to; a venture can be one mandate file.
- Research role: the built-in `research` lever sends a question to the command named under `research:` and keeps the answers as inferred facts with source and confidence, bounded and stripped to the record's shape.
- Parents: `children:` and `parent:`, loops refused at add; parent levers budget, pause, resume (declared-only) and threshold; shared quotas for asks, spend and sends, refused before the gate and shown by `why`; a parent's nightly call sees its children's records, not their raw input.
- Knowledge: `avsm know <venture|topic>` lists current facts by a Datalog query where superseding is a later record citing the old; summaries past `summarize_after` keep the context small and the ledger whole; a model may drill with know and why, and every drill is a record.
- Ratchet and digest: after `ratchet` approvals of one lever and payload shape, `self` proposes the mandate change that delegates it; approving rewrites the mandate and re-adopts it. `avsm digest`, written nightly, with asks and incidents a week.
- Tailscale access: `listen: tailscale`, bound to the tailnet address only, identity from tailscaled, logins under `remote_users:`; declared records carry the login; `avsm --remote host:port`.
- Hardening: `network:` per venture, held for adapters by the warden and for `run` by a network namespace where Linux allows one; ci.yml on every push to main; AGENTS.md states the warden extracts and never interprets.
- Examples: github-repo, stripe-store, mail-inbox, paper-trader, greenhouse, portfolio beside counter, each verified end to end with stubs; README for 1.0.

## 0.1.0 - 2026-10-15

- Ledger: append-only, hash-chained records in SQLite; every record is measured, declared, or inferred.
- Mandates: `mandate.yaml` says what a venture is for, what number to watch, what it may do, and what it may not.
- Gate and why: an action is accepted only if a proof exists from measured and declared facts under a mandate; `avsm why` prints the proof or the refusal.
- Unix socket and CLI: start, add, status, tail, asks, approve, deny, pause, resume, why. No public listener.
- Kernel: supervises the venture's process, reads its metric on a cadence, decides once a night; the warden and the roles run as separate processes, and only the warden holds credentials.
- Built-in `self` venture: cannot edit its own mandate, merge to main, or touch keys.
- examples/counter: the smallest venture, to start from.
- Release pipeline: a tag push tests, builds static musl binaries for x86_64 and aarch64, attaches them to the GitHub release, and publishes to crates.io.
